# Exercises — Chapter 4

Chapter: [Indexing](../chapters/04-indexing.md) · module: [`examples/ch04_indexing.py`](../examples/ch04_indexing.py) · solutions: [04](solutions/04.md)

**1. [measure]** Let `idx = np.array([0, 0, 0, 1, 1, 2])`. Add 1 at each index of `np.zeros(3, dtype=int)` in two ways.

- Use `a[idx] += 1`.
- Use `np.add.at`.

Report both results. Expected: `[1 1 1]` and `[3 2 1]`, exact.

**2. [break]** The `np.where(x > 0, np.log(x), 0)` call in chapter 4 gives a warning and not an error. Wrap the call so that it raises `FloatingPointError`, and say what that shows about `np.where`. Expected: the message `divide by zero encountered in log`.

**3. [judge]** A team uses `np.add.at`. They say that `np.bincount` is "known to be faster". Chapter 4 measured the two as within a factor of 2 on one version of NumPy. What does a good answer weigh before the team changes the code?

Consider the version, the key range, the profile of their own data, and the cost of the change.
